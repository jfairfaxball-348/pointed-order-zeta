## 2. Exact degree formula used

For the positive non-perfect-power bases used here, h=1 and the Wagstaff formula reproduced in Felix–Murty Proposition 3.3 specializes to
\[
[K_n(a):\mathbf Q]
=
\frac{n\varphi(n)}{\varepsilon_a(n)},
\]
where
\[
\varepsilon_a(n)=
\begin{cases}
2,&2\mid n\text{ and }d(a)\mid n,\\
1,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and d(a) is the discriminant of the quadratic field generated by the squarefree kernel of a.

The implementation deliberately raises NotImplementedError for perfect-power bases rather than inventing the omitted correction.

Negative bases are not used for theorem-driven comparisons because Session 3 did not source-match Felix–Murty's weighted theorem there.

## 3. Delta_a(z) by two independent routes

Route A uses exactly the frozen project normalization
\[
\Delta^{\rm prime}_{a,X}(z)
=
\frac1{\pi(X)}
\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\p\nmid a}}I_a(p)^{-z}.
\]

Route B uses
\[
\Delta^{\rm Kummer}_{a,N}(z)
=
\sum_{n\le N}
\frac{g_z(n)}{[K_n(a):\mathbf Q]}.
\]

## 4. Exact z=0 check

At z=0 the code verifies, for every cutoff containing the relevant prime divisors,
\[
Z_{a,X}(s,0)
\left(\prod_{p\le X}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\right)^{-1}
=
\prod_{\substack{p\mid a\\p\le X}}(1-p^{-s}).
\]
This is also a unit test. It confirms that the full-zeta normalization leaves precisely the expected finite omitted-prime factor.

## 5. Leading singularity diagnostic and the cutoff trap

A finite Z_{a,X} should not be combined naively with the full zeta(s) when s is close to 1. The missing prime tail of Z_{a,X} is then unmatched and can dominate the display.

The reproducible diagnostic instead uses the same prime cutoff in both products:
\[
\log H^{\rm match}_{a,X}(s,z)
=
\log Z_{a,X}(s,z)
-
\Delta^{\rm Kummer}_{a,N}(z)
\log\prod_{p\le X}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.
\]

For X=10^6 and N=5 times 10^5:

## 6. Prime-zeta versus zeta normalization

Numerically and analytically, the clean decomposition is
\[
\log H_a(s,z)
=
\bigl(P_a(s,z)-\Delta_a(z)P(s)\bigr)
+
\bigl(R_a(s,z)-\Delta_a(z)R_\zeta(s)\bigr).
\]
The second bracket starts at prime squares and converges rapidly. The first bracket is the slow, prime-supported part.

No additional deterministic infinite normalization was needed. Only the explicit finite set p|a distinguishes the full-zeta normalization from the omitted-prime zeta product.

## 7. Large z

For fixed cutoff, I_a(p)^{-z} approaches the indicator of I_a(p)=1. At a=2, s=1.1, X=10^6:
\[
\log Z_{\rm primitive}(1.1)=0.9998418846,
\]
while
\[
\log Z_{2,X}(1.1,4)=1.0246665987,
\qquad
\log Z_{2,X}(1.1,8)=1.0013465673.
\]
The finite product approaches the primitive-root prime product as expected. No interchange of z->infinity with the infinite Euler product is claimed.

## 8. Complex-z diagnostics

Weights are evaluated as
\[
I^{-z}=e^{-z\log I}
\]
using the real logarithm of the positive integer I, so there is no branch ambiguity in z.

For a=2:

- z=0.25+2i: Kummer N=5 times 10^5 gives 0.31863632-0.22746635i; the prime mean at X=10^6 gives 0.31854631-0.22802241i.
- z=0.05+5i: Kummer gives 0.14224038+0.10067144i; prime mean gives 0.14017451+0.10100864i.
- z=3i: Kummer gives 0.22275705-0.16564865i; prime mean gives 0.22155172-0.16698611i.

The analytic note proves absolute/local-uniform convergence of the positive non-perfect-power Kummer degree series for Re(z)>-1. These comparisons do not establish a complex-z prime-density theorem.

## 9. Numerical quality control

Every displayed experiment fixes or records:

- base a;
- z;
- s when relevant;
- prime cutoff X;
- Kummer cutoff N;
- exact degree formula;
- exclusion of p|a;
- arithmetic convention: exact integers for discrete arithmetic, binary64 complex values for transformed weights/logs;
- whether the value is a prime empirical mean, a Kummer partial sum, or a finite Euler-product diagnostic.

The default driver also writes compact CSV/JSON files when run locally. Large raw per-prime datasets are intentionally not required for reproduction.

## 10. Negative results

1. The normalized real boundary behavior is exactly what the known Felix–Murty error predicts; the computation does not create a new theorem.
2. Unmatched finite-Z/full-zeta normalization is misleading near s=1.
3. No stable secondary power or logarithmic term survived changes of cutoff, base, and z strongly enough to record as a conjecture.
4. No unexpectedly simple new constants emerged.
5. Complex z behaves smoothly but has not revealed arithmetic structure beyond the formulas already predict.
6. The Euler-product layer did not reveal information clearly absent from the centered weighted prime Dirichlet series.

## 11. Computational verdict

The experiments validate the implementation, source-matched degree formula, generalized-Artin mean, z=0 endpoint, and the expected Level-1 normalization. They provide no evidence strong enough for Level 2 or Level 3 continuation.

The computational result therefore supports the reframe:
\[
\boxed{
Q_a(s,z)=P_a(s,z)-\Delta_a(z)P(s)
}
\]
is the more fundamental analytic object. H_a remains a convenient packaging of Q_a plus a holomorphic prime-square remainder.
